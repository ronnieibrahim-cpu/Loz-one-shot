Complete Walkthrough

Title screen to the Drowned King, in eighteen chapters. Every dungeon room by room, every boss phase by phase, all 24 Heart Pieces, the eleven-link Coastwise Chain, and the exact tile to stand on for every puzzle in the game.

How to read a room key. d1/0,3,2 is the engine's own name for a screen — map, floor, x, y, with x running east and y running south. Tile coordinates inside a room are (x, y), x from 0–9 left to right and y from 0–7 top to bottom. Every fact here is read out of the game's data, so any of it can be checked against the source with those keys.

A and B are assignable slots, not fixed roles. Whatever you pick up first goes to B, the second to A, and you rearrange them from the pause menu. A fresh game starts with the Moon Conch on B and the sword on A.

Two verbs are not items and never will be. The hop — walk into a one-tile gap and Link hops it automatically; there is no feather in this game, and a gap two tiles wide is a wall. Lifting — on the A button, context first, so standing next to a pot with an item bound to A means the pot comes up.

The tide, and why it is not a switch

Sound the Moon Conch and the tide cycles LOW → MID → HIGH → LOW. Two presses reach any level from any other. That is the whole interface, and almost everything else in the game is about the fact that the tide is a field, not a global: the level that matters is the one under whatever you are asking about — your feet, an enemy's feet, a tile's. Once you own the Tidewright's Anchor you can hold one patch of a room at the level it had while the rest of the room moves, and your charms read that field too.

LevelWhat the world does
LOWSandbars, reef flats and seafloor exposed. Sunken cave mouths open, channels wadeable. Drains become open pits. Water wheels stop.
MIDThe default. Wells are over your head, sluices ankle deep.
HIGHShallows become deep water. Low walls drown and can be swum over. Rafts float up to high ledges. Drowned boles vanish.

In a room's text grid the digits 0–9 are always tide tiles. Anything that is not a digit does not move with the water — a rule with no exceptions anywhere in the game.

Health, damage and money

You start with 3 hearts. Health is quarter-hearts, four to a heart. There are 24 Heart Pieces, four to a container, and six Heart Containers — one per boss. Collect everything and you finish on 15 hearts.

Charms, in one paragraph

Charms are carved bone, slotted by tide level. You carry three cases and a charm only works while the water under your feet is at its case's level. You start with the MID case; the LOW case cuts itself at 2 Essences, the HIGH case at 4, and at 6 every case holds two. Seven charms are placed by hand; the other twenty-three come from the scrimshander, who takes a blank and 60 rupees and gives back whichever charm the bone wanted to be.

Part I

The route at a glance

The dungeons are numbered 1–6 and the recommended order is the numbered one: each boss is meaningfully harder than the last (24 → 30 → 36 → 44 → 52 → 80 HP) and each dungeon's puzzle grammar assumes the last one's item.

The Drowned Wood Shrine's door is open from the first minute. Nothing gates it — you can walk in with three hearts and a wooden sword, and Rootmaw has 52 HP. Dungeons 3 and 4 both need bombs, which come out of Dungeon 2.

The shop will take your rupees for ammunition it cannot give you. Bombs and Bottled Tide are both counted pickups that clamp to a capacity, and that capacity starts at zero. The shop refuses the sale outright until you own the bag (Dungeon 2's Bomb Vault) or the case (the Salt Pan Vault) — it says so rather than taking your money for nothing, but there is no point walking in early hoping to stock up.

The Reef Hollow — the Kilnshell

A dry column of sand runs up to the cave mouth on Sunken Reef from the south, so you can reach the mouth at any tide; the reef flats around it are sandbar — dry at LOW, wadeable at MID, deep at HIGH — so drop the water first if you want to explore the rest of that screen rather than walk straight in.

Reef Hollowcave2/0,0,0
  • A rupee cache at (3,3), inside the seafloor patch — walkable only at LOW. A readable at (8,3), across the room, is the whole puzzle: "When the sea withdraws, walk where fish swam."
  • Heart Piece 2 at (2,2), deeper in the same patch. Same requirement: LOW tide only.
  • A big chest at (7,5), in the open, no requirements — the Kilnshell.
  • A second rupee cache at (7,1), in a niche walled off by two tiles of drift-tangle (7,2 and 8,2) — nothing else in the game opens it. This is where the item's movement verb is taught, for twenty rupees rather than a dungeon.
  • A readable at (1,5), low on the wall by the tide pool: "Lime drinks the sea and spits fire. Drown it and it sulks."

How the Kilnshell works. Press the button and it sets one down, already lit, on the tile you're facing. Press again, from anywhere, to take it back. It burns everything it touches — torches, drift-tangle, and any enemy standing over it, a little damage per tick rather than one big hit, because it is a trap you place and the tide springs rather than a weapon you swing. Set it beside a torch (not on top of one — torches are solid) and it catches on its own.

The one thing the sea still has a say in: carry it into deep water and it goes out. Set it down and strike it again — the one note this item keeps from the rest of the roster. You can also pick it up the ordinary way, like a pot or a rock, and carry it further before throwing it back down.

It has to live here rather than in a dungeon: fire is the one verb nothing else in the game can perform, and the Coral Spire's Torch Cell — the first puzzle that needs it — is the sixth room of the second dungeon.

The Sluicegate — the Anchor

The Sluicegated1/0,3,2

The big chest at (4,4) is the Tidewright's Anchor.

"Iron remembers. Sink it where you want the sea to stay."

How it works. Throw it and it sinks: within roughly eight tiles the tide stays at whatever level it was when the iron landed, while the rest of the room obeys the conch. Press again to recall. The chain sweeps for damage on both throw and recall. Two things follow that are not obvious: the held patch is five tiles across, which is why the gate rooms are the width they are; and your charms read the field too, so standing inside a held patch keeps that patch's charms awake.

The boss door is directly above you in this room. The Boss Key is in the west wing.

The gate primitive, stated once because three rooms are built from it. Written along a row from the side you arrive on:

.  3 3 3 3  4 4 4  .
3 a well — wadeable at LOW, over your head above. 4 a drain — an open pit at LOW, water above it.

Nothing between the two bands is walkable at both levels, and the hop clears two tiles, so neither band is in range. The answer:

  • Wells near you — sound LOW, sink the iron in the well, sound to MID. The drain ahead fills while your well stays walkable.
  • Drains near you — sound MID, sink the iron in the drain, sound to LOW. The well ahead empties while your drain keeps its water.

Chapter 04 · Interlude

The Maku Tree, the shop, the Chain begins

Go to houseMaku/0,0,0. With one Essence the Maku Tree is awake enough to work — but she wants something of the Bell's as well, and that is the Bell-Rope at the end of the Coastwise Chain.

Do not skip this

The Resonance Rod is the Chain's reward, and the Rod is what opens the Salt Pans. You cannot finish the game without going into the Pans — the Bottled Tide case, two Heart Pieces and the Reef Palace are all in there. Run the Chain.

  • Buy the shield (30 rupees) if you have not.
  • Commission a charm. The scrimshander takes your blank and 60 rupees. It is ready after three turns of the tide — sound the conch three times and go back. The charm is rolled from every charm you do not own; you cannot choose.
  • Bombs are 20 rupees for four — but you have nothing to carry them in yet. The pouch is in Dungeon 2. Wait.

The First Forkd2/1,4,3PINNED LOW

The room pins the tide to LOW and refuses the conch. The only thing that moves water in here is the coral sluice at the bottom of each alcove — and by the time you can reach one you have taken a one-way ledge you cannot climb back up. Read the shelf at (4,5), then commit.

Boss · Dungeon 2 Anemos, the Crowned Column
30 HP d2/1,3,1 FLOOD THE SPIRE

Rooted to the floor, so this is a fight about position rather than pursuit. It fills the room with shots and lashes anything that stands next to it.

Anemos has to unfurl to feed, and it feeds on what the water brings. Its open window is 40 frames at LOW, 80 at MID, 160 at HIGH. The feed cycle runs every 250 frames regardless, so at HIGH you get roughly two and a half seconds of open target every four seconds.

How the Rod works. How it works. Cast it into deep water and drag. Three separate behaviours, all of which matter:

  • A fixed snag pulls you. Cast at a mooring post and the Line hauls you to it, over anything — a pit included. This is the crossing verb.
  • The floor is searchable. Dragged back across silted ground it brings up what the floor is holding — and blanks are common in dredged loot and rare everywhere else, which is how you supply the scrimshander.
  • It drags aquatic enemies onto land, where they flop and are helpless.

Two guards define its geometry: you cannot cast while swimming or on the seafloor (a weighted line is thrown from your heels), and the cast stops dead on solid tiles — a lintel of the Keep's masonry across a shaft blocks the line until the sea covers it.

And the rule that makes the Keep the Keep: the floor gives up what it is holding only while the sea is on it. A silted cache is searchable only at the level where its tile reads wet. Chapter 17 · The final fight

Nereth, the Drowned King

"I did not break the Bell to be cruel, wader. I broke it because the sea was told what to do for a thousand years and never once asked."

Boss · Dungeon 6 Nereth, the Drowned King
80 HP d6/1,3,1 BREAK THE PIN

The fight is about the conch, entirely. Each of the first three phases Nereth pins the tide to one level, and while it sits where he put it he is sealed inside his own water and nothing touches him. Sound the conch off his level and he is open until he can shove it back — on a timer you can hear coming, since the re-pin has a 26-frame wind-up.

He is never only breakable by the conch: every attack in his first three phases ends with a recovery window of about 55 frames. So you can fight him badly and still win, slowly. Fight him with the conch and you win three times as fast.

Bring Bottled Tides. He is the one thing in the game that fights you for the tide continuously, and a bottle is a free step when he has just re-pinned and you are out of position.

1

above 75% · pinned MID Chases at 0.5 and throws three-trident spreads every 130 frames after a 22-frame wind-up. Sound the conch to LOW or HIGH and he opens. If you are stuck in the pin, punish the trident recovery.

2

75–50% · pinned HIGH Floods the keep and swims it, fast (1.05), ringing the room with ten-bubble rings every 150 frames. A wizzrobe at the phase change, topped up to two. Think about which single press you actually want.

3

50–25% · pinned LOW Drains the keep to bare rock, plants the trident, and sweeps beams continuously — three beams every 22 frames, each volley rotated, raking the whole room. The sweep never stops, so the opening is when he re-seats the trident every 190 frames, with a 20-frame tell. Two stalfos, topped up to three.

4

below 25% · no pin He works the sea through all three states himself, cycling every 200 frames — and the moment the water is moving, so is he: open for 150 frames after every cycle. Five-trident spreads every 100 frames, twelve-bubble rings every 170, keese in pairs up to four. This is the phase that kills people. The windows are enormous; so is the ordnance in the air. Stay at range, walk the ring gaps, go in hard every time the sea turns over.

Reward: Heart Container, then the last shard comes away from his crown — Essence VI — the Drowned King's Bell. The six find each other in your hands and the Tide Bell is whole. It is much smaller than the stories, and much heavier.

Six Essences upgrades every charm case to hold two. If you are completing the game rather than finishing it, this is the moment to go back and rebuild all three loadouts.

Charms and the scrimshander

Thirty charms exist. Seven are placed by hand; the other twenty-three come out of the scrimshander's bench.

How to get one carved. Bring her a blank — raw bone, shell, anything the sea has finished with — and 60 rupees. She chooses what it becomes. Ready after three turns of the tide: sound the conch three times and come back.

Where blanks come from. Three are placed in the world — d1/0,2,6, d2/0,2,6, d3/0,4,6. After that they are a rare drop from tougher enemies, and a common one in dredged loot, which is what the Dredge Line is really for if you want all thirty.

How this was verified

Everything above is read out of the shipped data. The room keys, chest contents, enemy placements, key counts, puzzle coordinates, boss HP totals, phase thresholds, attack timers and tide hooks are all taken from the game's source, not from the design documents.

The route claims are backed by the project's own checkers: the progression flood reaches 120/120 screens with 6/6 dungeons cleared in acquisition order; the overworld checker proves each gate seals its region and opens with its key; six per-item checkers prove that the puzzle rooms in their dungeon cannot be crossed without that dungeon's item and can be with it — which is where the exact stand here, face this way, at this tide solutions come from; the heart census counts the pieces and the cap; the trade checker plays the Coastwise Chain end to end in-engine, then rings the Colonnade's grate with the Rod that comes out of it; and the torch checker confirms something in the engine actually emits the fire action the Kilnshell relies on, and that no torch-gated key is ever the only key standing between a player and the room it opens — the Coral Spire's Torch Cell key is deliberately redundant with Rising Chamber's for exactly that reason.

Two honest caveats

  1. No automated run has yet played this game from the title screen to Nereth. The playthrough harness is the only tool that plays rather than models, and it currently drives 18 of the game's 144 dungeon rooms, all in Dungeon 1, ending in d1/0,5,2 after crossing the Iron Pipe with the Anchor. Every model says the world is completable and none of them fights a boss or spends a key. This walkthrough is therefore derived correctly from the data and, past that point, not yet walked — treat it as a confident route, not as a claim that anyone has taken it end to end.
  2. The ending cutscene is written and is not wired up. It exists in full — the Bell rings, the sea goes back where it belongs, Farore signs off, the credits roll — and nothing starts it. What actually happens when Nereth dies is the Heart Container, then the sixth Essence scene, and then you are standing in the arena with a whole Tide Bell and no curtain.

Neither is a fault in the world this guide describes — the rooms, the items, the puzzles and the bosses are all there and all consistent, structurally verified end to end. They are things you should know before you set out to see the end of it.
