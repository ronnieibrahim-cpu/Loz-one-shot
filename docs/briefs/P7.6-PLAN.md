# P7.6 — Multi-screen dungeon rooms: the plan

**Status: PLAN ONLY. No code written.** The brief says "use plan mode and show
me the plan before you touch code", so this session stopped here deliberately.
Approve or amend, and the next session executes it.

---

## The one encouraging finding from the survey

`ROOM_W` / `ROOM_H` / `VIEW_W` / `VIEW_H` appear **30 times across six files**,
and every single use falls into one of three meanings:

| Meaning | Example | Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| the room's size in tiles | `this.base[ty * ROOM_W + tx]`, `inBounds` | `room.tw` / `room.th` |
| the room's size in pixels | `px >= VIEW_W` in `canOccupy`, `checkSwordClink` | `room.pw` / `room.ph` |
| the size of the window on screen | the clip rect in `draw()`, the HUD | stays `VIEW_W` / `VIEW_H` |

The engine already treats "the room's extent" as one concept — it has just been
spelled with the viewport constant, because until now they were equal. **The
work is mostly separating those three meanings, not inventing a camera.** That
is why this is tractable in one session despite the "high" rating.

`drawScene(ctx, ox, oy)` already takes a pixel offset (the screen shake uses
it), so the camera enters as another term in `ox`/`oy` rather than as a new
rendering path.

Two things I checked and want on the record because they look like bugs and are
not: `checkSwordClink` compares `tx`/`ty` against `VIEW_W`/`VIEW_H`, but those
locals are PIXELS despite the names, so it is correct; and `tileDefAt` does
multiply by `TILE` before comparing. Neither is a latent defect. Do not "fix"
them.

## Step 1 — Room gains a size, structurally

`Room` gains `sw`/`sh` (screens, from `def.size` or `[1,1]`) and the derived
`tw = sw * ROOM_W`, `th = sh * ROOM_H`, `pw = tw * TILE`, `ph = th * TILE`.
The flat `base`/`override` arrays index on `ty * this.tw + tx`.

**The size must be refused outside the allowed set** — 1x1, 2x1, 1x2, 2x2, 3x1
— at construction, throwing rather than warning. An unbounded room size is a
different game.

**Making 1x1 structural rather than conventional** (the brief's word) means:
`registerMap` rejects a `size` on any map whose `kind` is `'overworld'`, and
`validate.mjs` asserts it. Not a comment saying overworld rooms are 1x1 — a
check that fails.

Grid parsing: a room's `map` is an array of strings. A 2x1 room is 20 chars
wide and 8 tall; a 1x2 room is 10 wide and 16 tall. The parser asserts the grid
matches the declared size exactly, which is what stops a 3x1 room being
authored as three 1x1 grids by accident.

**Backward compatibility is free**: no `size` means `[1,1]`, `tw` is `ROOM_W`,
and every existing grid parses byte-identically.

## Step 2 — The camera

New `src/game/camera.js`, owned by `Game`, holding `cx`/`cy` in pixels, clamped
to `[0, room.pw - VIEW_W]` and `[0, room.ph - VIEW_H]`. A 1x1 room clamps both
to 0, so the camera is provably a no-op there — that is the mechanism by which
"nothing may alter a single pixel in a 1x1 room" is guaranteed rather than
hoped for.

**Deadzone, not centring.** The source holds the camera still until Link nears
an edge, then moves. So: a box in view space; while Link is inside it the
camera does not move; when he leaves, the camera moves by exactly enough to put
him back on the boundary. New in `feel.js`, all `guessed`:
`CAM_DEADZONE_W`, `CAM_DEADZONE_H`, and `CAM_MAX_SPEED` (px/f, so the camera
cannot snap when Link is knocked back or warped).

**Debug key** to draw the deadzone box and the camera window, on the same
argument as `KeyO` for the anchor: this is settled by play, not by reasoning.
`KeyI` is free.

Note the interaction to get right first time: the camera must update AFTER the
player moves and BEFORE anything draws, and it must not update during a tide
sweep (the sweep snapshots the room and a moving camera would smear the wipe).

## Step 3 — Rendering

`Room.render` currently returns one `VIEW_W x VIEW_H` canvas. It becomes a
`pw x ph` canvas — 480x128 at the largest — and `drawScene` blits the camera
window out of it. `drawAnim`/`drawOver` already take `ox, oy`; they get the
camera offset like everything else.

**The cache key does not change and must not.** P5's `cacheKeyFor(tide)` stays
exactly as it is: the camera is not part of the key, because moving the camera
does not change what the room looks like. Getting this wrong in either
direction is the trap — keying on the camera would re-render every frame you
walk, and letting the camera invalidate the cache would defeat the stamp
discipline P5 built. **The camera must never touch `invalidate()`.**

Memory: a 3x1 room's cache is 480x128, under four times a screen. Fine. But
`renderAt` (the Lens) allocates a second one, so a Lens L2 in a 3x1 room holds
three full-room canvases. Worth measuring, not worth pre-optimising.

## Step 4 — Transitions, the most likely bug in the session

`checkRoomExit` currently reads:

```js
if (i.down('right') && r.x + r.w >= VIEW_W - M) dir = 'right';
```

`VIEW_W` here means "the right-hand edge of the room" and must become
`room.pw`. All four cardinals, same change. That single substitution is the
whole of item 4 — an internal screen seam is simply not a boundary any more,
because nothing was ever checking for a seam, only for the room's extent.

The brief is right that this is where the bug will be, but I expect it in the
places that ASSUME a transition means one screen of travel:
`updateTransition`'s slide distance, `ROOM_EXIT_MARGIN`'s re-entry position on
the far side, and `respawnAnchor`/`restoreRoomState` keyed on room identity.
The proof is the brief's: walk a 2x1 room end to end, assert exactly one
transition.

## Step 5 — The checkers

- `validate.mjs` — grid dimensions match the declared size; overworld rooms
  declare none; the size is in the allowed set.
- `walk-dungeons.mjs`, `solve-switches.mjs`, `find-ledges.mjs` — every
  `for (x < ROOM_W)` becomes `room.tw`. Mechanical, and the flood fill is
  already room-scoped so connectivity needs no rethink.
- `check-overworld.mjs` — unchanged by construction, which is itself the
  assertion that the overworld path did not move.
- The P5 anchor strand check — **the anchor's disc does not grow**. The brief
  states this is a design consequence, wanted, not a bug. So the checker must
  keep asserting "the anchor cannot strand you" over the larger room, and it
  will legitimately find placements in a 3x1 room that a 1x1 room never
  offered. Do not compensate by scaling the radius.

## Step 6 — Chartstone and the minimap

`menu.js`'s `tideMarks` walks `ROOM_W x ROOM_H` and caches per room key — it
becomes `room.tw x room.th`, and the cache key is already the room key so a
multi-screen room is one entry. The minimap draws a multi-screen room as one
cell spanning `sw x sh` grid cells, as the source's dungeon maps do, which
means the map screen's grid iteration must skip cells already covered by a
room that started earlier. That is the fiddliest drawing in the session.

## Step 7 — One room converted, and the replay

Convert exactly one existing dungeon room to 2x1. **Candidate: a D1 corridor
room**, chosen after running `find-ledges.mjs` and `walk-dungeons.mjs` to pick
one whose connectivity is simple and whose neighbours' seams are plain wall —
converting a room changes what the room to its east must have facing it.

New replay `wide-room-walk`: enter, walk east end to end, assert exactly one
transition fired and the camera reached its clamp at both ends.

## What I would NOT do in this session

- Convert any other room. P8 does that with design intent.
- Support arbitrary sizes.
- Touch the overworld path at all.
- Use the P7.5 tilesets. That is P8's, and P7.5's tiledef pass is blocked on
  assets anyway (see `docs/ART-BACKLOG.md`).

## Order of work, and where to stop if it runs long

1. Room size + parser + validate (safe, reversible, proves 1x1 unchanged).
2. Camera + deadzone + debug key.
3. Render cache widening.
4. `checkRoomExit` and the transition audit.
5. Checkers.
6. Convert one room + replay.
7. Minimap.

**Steps 1–4 are the session.** If time runs short, stop after 4 with no room
converted: the engine can then express a multi-screen room and nothing in the
game uses one, which is a coherent state to hand over. Stopping mid-5 with a
converted room and a checker that cannot walk it is not.
