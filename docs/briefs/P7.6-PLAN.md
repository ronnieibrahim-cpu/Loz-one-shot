# P7.6 — Multi-screen dungeon rooms: the plan

**Status: APPROVED WITH AMENDMENTS, still no engine code written.** All ten
review points are folded in below and marked **[A1]**-**[A10]**. Two of them
corrected the plan's facts; one corrected the reviewer's, and that is called out
rather than quietly followed — see [A1] and [A6].

---

## The one encouraging finding from the survey

`ROOM_W` / `ROOM_H` / `VIEW_W` / `VIEW_H` appear **30 times across six files**,
and every single use falls into one of three meanings:

| Meaning | Example | Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| the room's size in tiles | `this.base[ty * ROOM_W + tx]`, `inBounds` | `room.tw` / `room.th` |
| the room's size in pixels | `px >= VIEW_W` in `canOccupy`, `checkSwordClink` | `room.pw` / `room.ph` |
| the size of the window on screen | the clip rect in `draw()`, the HUD | stays `VIEW_W` / `VIEW_H` |

The engine already treats "the room's extent" as one concept — it has just been
spelled with the viewport constant, because until now they were equal. **The
work is mostly separating those three meanings, not inventing a camera.** That
is why this is tractable in one session despite the "high" rating.

`drawScene(ctx, ox, oy)` already takes a pixel offset (the screen shake uses
it), so the camera enters as another term in `ox`/`oy` rather than as a new
rendering path.

Two things I checked and want on the record because they look like bugs and are
not: `checkSwordClink` compares `tx`/`ty` against `VIEW_W`/`VIEW_H`, but those
locals are PIXELS despite the names, so it is correct; and `tileDefAt` does
multiply by `TILE` before comparing. Neither is a latent defect. Do not "fix"
them.

## Step 1 — Room gains a size, structurally

`Room` gains `sw`/`sh` (screens, from `def.size` or `[1,1]`) and the derived
`tw = sw * ROOM_W`, `th = sh * ROOM_H`, `pw = tw * TILE`, `ph = th * TILE`.
The flat `base`/`override` arrays index on `ty * this.tw + tx`.

**The size must be refused outside the allowed set** — 1x1, 2x1, 1x2, 2x2, 3x1
— at construction, throwing rather than warning. An unbounded room size is a
different game.

**Making 1x1 structural rather than conventional** (the brief's word) means:
`registerMap` rejects a `size` on any map whose `kind` is `'overworld'`, and
`validate.mjs` asserts it. Not a comment saying overworld rooms are 1x1 — a
check that fails.

Grid parsing: a room's `map` is an array of strings. A 2x1 room is 20 chars
wide and 8 tall; a 1x2 room is 10 wide and 16 tall. The parser asserts the grid
matches the declared size exactly, which is what stops a 3x1 room being
authored as three 1x1 grids by accident.

**Backward compatibility is free**: no `size` means `[1,1]`, `tw` is `ROOM_W`,
and every existing grid parses byte-identically.

## Step 2 — The camera

New `src/game/camera.js`, owned by `Game`, holding `cx`/`cy` in pixels, clamped
to `[0, room.pw - VIEW_W]` and `[0, room.ph - VIEW_H]`. A 1x1 room clamps both
to 0, so the camera is provably a no-op there — that is the mechanism by which
"nothing may alter a single pixel in a 1x1 room" is guaranteed rather than
hoped for.

**Deadzone, not centring.** The source holds the camera still until Link nears
an edge, then moves. So: a box in view space; while Link is inside it the
camera does not move; when he leaves, the camera moves by exactly enough to put
him back on the boundary. New in `feel.js`: `CAM_DEADZONE_W`, `CAM_DEADZONE_H`,
and `CAM_MAX_SPEED` (px/f, so the camera cannot snap when Link is knocked back
or warped).

**[A4] These are `guessed`, and the reference EXISTS.** An earlier draft of this
plan implied there was nothing to measure against. That is false and must not be
repeated in FEEL-SPEC: the source has exactly this camera in exactly these
rooms — the wide halls of Poison Moth's Lair and the lower corridors of Ancient
Ruins. If the session does not frame-step one of those, every camera constant
ships tagged `guessed` with the words **"not yet measured, reference exists"**
in `docs/FEEL-SPEC.md`, naming those two rooms. Never write that no reference
exists. `measured` remains earnable here, unlike the scrimshaw constants.

**[A7] The camera SNAPS, it does not glide, on `setRoom` and on respawn.**
`CAM_MAX_SPEED` governs play only. A camera that eases into position on a room
entry would show the player a pan they did not ask for, and on respawn it would
animate away from the death they just watched. Both call a `snap()` that sets
`cx`/`cy` to the clamped target outright.

**[A1] Debug key — the plan's `KeyI` was wrong, and so is the review's `KeyU`.**
The plan said "KeyI is free"; the review said KeyI belongs to the anchor-radius
cycle and to use KeyU instead. Both are wrong, and the code is the authority.
`src/core/input.js` only forwards SIX codes to `takeExtra()`, and
`src/game/game.js` consumes four of them:

| Code | Reaches `extra`? | Handler |
|---|---|---|
| `KeyP` | yes | mute |
| `KeyO` | yes | debug overlay |
| `KeyU` | yes | **`cycleAnchorRadius` (P5)** |
| `KeyY` | yes | `cycleAnchorShape` (P5) |
| `KeyF` | yes | **none — dead entry** |
| `KeyR` | yes | **none — dead entry** |
| `KeyI` | **no** | never reaches `takeExtra` at all |

So `KeyU` would collide head-on with the anchor radius, which is itself an
unsettled design constant that still needs a human with that key. And `KeyI`
would not have failed loudly — it would have silently done nothing, because it
is not in the whitelist.

**Use `KeyC`** (mnemonic: camera), added to the `input.js` whitelist in the same
commit. `KeyF` and `KeyR` are free today but read as fullscreen and reset, and
taking one would be borrowing a key someone is going to want. Do not add a debug
key without also adding it to that whitelist; the plan's original `KeyI` is the
worked example of how that fails quietly.

Note the interaction to get right first time: the camera must update AFTER the
player moves and BEFORE anything draws, and it must not update during a tide
sweep (the sweep snapshots the room and a moving camera would smear the wipe).
`Game.update` already returns early for the whole sweep, so the player cannot
move during one either — the two facts agree and neither should be relaxed
without the other.

**[A6] Screen shake and the camera both enter through `drawScene`'s `ox`/`oy`,
and the sum can sample past the room canvas.** The review is right about the
mechanism and wrong about one detail worth stating, because it changes the fix:
**this is not impossible in a 1x1 room, it happens there today.** `drawScene`
does `ctx.drawImage(base, ox, oy)` where `base` is exactly `VIEW_W x VIEW_H`,
and the shake offsets `ox` by up to `SHAKE_LARGE`, so a shaking 1x1 room already
exposes a few pixels of nothing along the trailing edge, inside the clip. It is
brief and nobody has reported it, which is not the same as it not happening.

Two fixes were offered — clamp the sum, or bleed a tile. **Clamping the sum is
wrong**: in a 1x1 room the camera is pinned at 0, so clamping would drive the
shake to zero and delete the effect from every room in the game.

The fix is to give the gap a colour. Fill the viewport with the room's void
colour immediately after the clip and before the blit. That changes only pixels
that were previously undefined, so it alters nothing in any room that is not
shaking — which keeps the "not a single pixel in a 1x1 room" constraint honest —
and during a shake it replaces black-that-means-nothing with black-that-means-
wall. The camera then needs no special case at all.

## [A3] Off-camera entities — freeze, and why it is not a preference

The plan omitted this entirely and it is not a detail: it decides whether the
replays survive.

**Entities outside the camera window FREEZE.** They are not updated, not
despawned, and not culled from the list. A frozen entity keeps its position, its
timers and its RNG state exactly as they were.

The reason is P4. A ground enemy draws from the seeded per-room stream to decide
its direction, and `tools/replay.mjs` asserts `game.rng.draws` at every
checkpoint. A 3x1 room holds roughly three screens of enemies, so "update
everything" would draw from that stream about three times as often as a 1x1
room — and every existing replay's draw count would be wrong for reasons that
have nothing to do with what the player did. Freezing keeps the number of draws
a function of what is on screen, which is what it has always been.

Despawning is worse: it is not reversible, and an enemy that vanished because
you walked away has taken its drop with it.

**The boundary must be deterministic and must not be the camera's floating
position.** Use the camera window expanded by `CAM_ACTIVATE_MARGIN` (new in
`feel.js`, `guessed`, in whole tiles), tested against the entity's tile — not
its pixel — so the set of live entities changes only when something crosses a
tile boundary. A margin in pixels against a camera that moves sub-pixel would
make liveness jitter on a diagonal, and P4's lattice would step an enemy in and
out of the simulation on alternate frames.

Bosses are exempt: `e.isBoss` always updates. A boss whose arena is 2x2 must not
stop thinking because the player backed into a corner — and that exemption is
also why step 8's audit has to happen before any boss room is converted.

## Step 3 — Rendering

`Room.render` currently returns one `VIEW_W x VIEW_H` canvas. It becomes a
`pw x ph` canvas — 480x128 at the largest — and `drawScene` blits the camera
window out of it. `drawAnim`/`drawOver` already take `ox, oy`; they get the
camera offset like everything else.

**The cache key does not change and must not.** P5's `cacheKeyFor(tide)` stays
exactly as it is: the camera is not part of the key, because moving the camera
does not change what the room looks like. Getting this wrong in either
direction is the trap — keying on the camera would re-render every frame you
walk, and letting the camera invalidate the cache would defeat the stamp
discipline P5 built. **The camera must never touch `invalidate()`.**

Memory: a 3x1 room's cache is 480x128, under four times a screen. Fine. But
`renderAt` (the Lens) allocates a second one, so a Lens L2 in a 3x1 room holds
three full-room canvases. Worth measuring, not worth pre-optimising.

### [A8] `drawSweep` stays SCREEN-space, and the Lens follows it

Resolved, with the reason, because it is the kind of thing a later session will
flip on aesthetic grounds:

`Tide.drawSweep` snapshots into a `VIEW_W x VIEW_H` offscreen and runs its wave
front across `VIEW_W + 40`. **Keep that.** The sweep is a picture of what the
player can see, wiped across the screen they are looking at.

Making it room-space would make the wipe's duration a function of room size: a
3x1 room would take three times as long, most of the front would travel where
nobody is looking, and `TIDE_SWEEP_FRAMES` would stop describing anything. That
constant is 23 precisely because P2 measured it against the screen — a
room-space sweep would re-introduce, geometrically, the exact defect P2 fixed.

Two consequences to implement rather than discover:
- the snapshot is taken of the CURRENT CAMERA WINDOW, not of the room origin;
- the camera is frozen for the whole sweep (already required above), so the
  window the snapshot describes is still the window when the front lands.

The Brineglass Lens takes the same answer for the same reason: `renderAt`
produces a room-sized canvas, and `drawLensGhost` blits the camera window out of
it. The overlay is glass held in front of the SCREEN.

## Step 4 — Transitions, the most likely bug in the session

`checkRoomExit` currently reads:

```js
if (i.down('right') && r.x + r.w >= VIEW_W - M) dir = 'right';
```

`VIEW_W` here means "the right-hand edge of the room" and must become
`room.pw`. All four cardinals, same change. That single substitution is the
whole of item 4 — an internal screen seam is simply not a boundary any more,
because nothing was ever checking for a seam, only for the room's extent.

The brief is right that this is where the bug will be, but I expect it in the
places that ASSUME a transition means one screen of travel:
`updateTransition`'s slide distance, `ROOM_EXIT_MARGIN`'s re-entry position on
the far side, and `respawnAnchor`/`restoreRoomState` keyed on room identity.
The proof is the brief's: walk a 2x1 room end to end, assert exactly one
transition.

**[A5] The slide is viewport-sized and slides THE CAMERA WINDOW AS OF THE
INSTANT OF EXIT — not the room, and not the room's origin.** A transition moves
one screen of pixels regardless of how big either room is.

The window at that instant is guaranteed to be the room's clamp, and the
guarantee has exactly one source: **the exit fires at `room.pw`**, so Link is
flush against the room's far edge, so the camera — which clamps to
`[0, room.pw - VIEW_W]` — is necessarily at its maximum. Nothing else enforces
this.

**That makes it fragile in a specific, nameable way.** If the deadzone ever
leaves the camera short of its clamp at the moment of exit, the outgoing half of
the slide shows a window that is not flush with the room edge, and the seam
TEARS — the two halves do not meet. Any future change to the deadzone, to
`CAM_MAX_SPEED`, or to `ROOM_EXIT_MARGIN` can cause it, and none of them looks
like it is about transitions.

So: assert it. On beginning a transition, the camera must already equal its
clamp on the axis being crossed, and that is a hard assertion in the engine, not
a checker-only one. It costs one comparison and it converts a rare visual tear
into an immediate, named failure.

## Step 5 — The checkers

- `validate.mjs` — grid dimensions match the declared size; overworld rooms
  declare none; the size is in the allowed set.
- **[A2] NO TWO ROOMS MAY CLAIM THE SAME GRID CELL. Hard failure, not a
  warning.** This is the check that makes the whole size mechanism safe and the
  plan did not have it. A 2x1 room keyed at `(3,4)` occupies `(3,4)` AND
  `(4,4)`; any room keyed to a cell already covered by another room's span is an
  error, and so is a span that runs off the edge of the map's 8x8 grid.

  Without it the failure is silent and awful: two rooms answer to one cell,
  `getRoom` returns whichever the Map happened to store, and which one you get
  depends on authoring order. The room would render, validate, and walk — and a
  door on the shared seam would lead somewhere that depends on a hash. Build the
  occupancy grid per map per floor, mark every cell of every room's span, and
  fail naming both room keys and the contested cell.

  It also has to run BEFORE the connectivity checkers, because a stranded-room
  report is meaningless if two rooms are fighting over a cell.
- `walk-dungeons.mjs`, `solve-switches.mjs`, `find-ledges.mjs` — every
  `for (x < ROOM_W)` becomes `room.tw`. Mechanical, and the flood fill is
  already room-scoped so connectivity needs no rethink.
- `check-overworld.mjs` — unchanged by construction, which is itself the
  assertion that the overworld path did not move.
- The P5 anchor strand check — **the anchor's disc does not grow**. The brief
  states this is a design consequence, wanted, not a bug. So the checker must
  keep asserting "the anchor cannot strand you" over the larger room, and it
  will legitimately find placements in a 3x1 room that a 1x1 room never
  offered. Do not compensate by scaling the radius.

## Step 6 — Chartstone and the minimap

`menu.js`'s `tideMarks` walks `ROOM_W x ROOM_H` and caches per room key — it
becomes `room.tw x room.th`, and the cache key is already the room key so a
multi-screen room is one entry. The minimap draws a multi-screen room as one
cell spanning `sw x sh` grid cells, as the source's dungeon maps do, which
means the map screen's grid iteration must skip cells already covered by a
room that started earlier. That is the fiddliest drawing in the session.

## Step 7 — One room converted, and the replay

Convert exactly one existing dungeon room to 2x1. **Candidate: a D1 corridor
room**, chosen after running `find-ledges.mjs` and `walk-dungeons.mjs` to pick
one whose connectivity is simple and whose neighbours' seams are plain wall —
converting a room changes what the room to its east must have facing it.

New replay `wide-room-walk`: enter, walk east end to end, assert exactly one
transition fired and the camera reached its clamp at both ends.

**[A9] It must ALSO assert the camera did not move while Link was inside the
deadzone.** "Reached its clamp at both ends" is necessary and nowhere near
sufficient — a plain centring camera with no deadzone at all passes it, and so
does a camera that jitters every frame. The deadzone is the whole fidelity
claim of step 2, and that assertion is the only thing that tests it.

Concretely: record `cam.cx` per frame alongside the existing checkpoint data,
and assert there exists a run of frames in which Link moved and `cx` did not
change at all. Then assert the converse — that `cx` did change once he left the
box — so a camera welded in place fails too.

## [A10] What this session must NOT settle

The Tidewright's Anchor's held patch does not scale with the room, and in a
large room that stops being a puzzle. A radius-2 disc is 13 tiles: 16% of a
10x8 room, where it splits the space, and about 5% of a 3x1 room, where it
splits nothing.

**Do not change the engine for this.** It is recorded as an OPEN DESIGN QUESTION
for P8 in `docs/NEXT-SESSION.md`, with the three options that were identified —
large rooms simply do not use the Anchor; the radius scales with room size; or
large rooms permit two anchors at once. Each has a different cost, one of them
(two anchors) is known to blow up `check-overworld`'s field flood, and picking
between them is a design call that wants the rooms to exist first.

## What I would NOT do in this session

- Convert any other room. P8 does that with design intent.
- Support arbitrary sizes.
- Touch the overworld path at all.
- Use the P7.5 tilesets. That is P8's, and P7.5's tiledef pass is blocked on
  assets anyway (see `docs/ART-BACKLOG.md`).

## Order of work, and where to stop if it runs long

1. Room size + parser + validate (safe, reversible, proves 1x1 unchanged).
2. Camera + deadzone + debug key.
3. Render cache widening.
4. `checkRoomExit` and the transition audit.
5. Checkers.
6. Convert one room + replay.
7. Minimap.

**Steps 1–4 are the session.** If time runs short, stop after 4 with no room
converted: the engine can then express a multi-screen room and nothing in the
game uses one, which is a coherent state to hand over. Stopping mid-5 with a
converted room and a checker that cannot walk it is not.
