WHY THIS MATTERS, in one paragraph, because it changes what "done" means here.
D1 is built around the Tidewright's Anchor. The Anchor holds a 5x5 patch of the
tide and its throw carries about two tiles, so ONE anchor gate needs a stand
tile, a four-tile band, a three-tile band and a far side — the full width of a
10-tile room row — and the rest of the room has to be walled off, because any
other route round the gate is a route that skips it and any forgiving tile in
the middle of it is somewhere to stand and sound the conch. That is why all
three of D1's anchor gates are bare two-row corridors and why no room in the
dungeon holds two of them, or holds one and anything else. A 2x1 room is twenty
tiles wide. THAT is what this session buys: the difference between the game's
keystone item having room for one idea per room and having room for a puzzle.
D3 (Cleats) and D4 (Bellows) are both spatial items and will hit the same wall.

SCOPE. The plan's seven steps, in its order. Steps 1-4 are the session; if time

THE CONSTRAINTS THAT MUST NOT MOVE, from the plan and the original brief:
  - All four existing replays pass UNCHANGED. Nothing here may alter a single
    pixel in a 1x1 room, and the camera clamping to 0 in a 1x1 room is the
    mechanism that guarantees it rather than the hope that does.
  - The overworld path is byte-identical. Overworld rooms may not declare a
    size, and that is a check that FAILS, not a comment.
  - An existing 10x8 grid with no declared size is a 1x1 room.
  - The room render cache key does not change. The camera is not part of it and
    must never call `invalidate()`. Both directions of that are traps.
  - The anchor's held patch does not grow with the room. A radius that split a
    10x8 room will split a 20x8 room differently; that is a design consequence
    the brief asked for, not a bug to compensate for.
  - Only 1x1, 2x1, 1x2, 2x2, 3x1. A size outside that set throws.

## If the session runs long, this is the order to shed

The plan already says steps 1–4 are the session. On top of that:

1. **Never shed addition B.** A capability no dungeon session knows how to use
   is the one outcome that makes this session worthless. Twenty minutes of
   writing at the end is cheaper than the whole feature being ignored.
2. Addition A is not optional either, but it is small — it is `W`/`H` becoming
   two fields.
3. The minimap (step 6) is the fiddliest drawing and the least load-bearing.
   Shed that first.
4. Then the converted room and its replay (step 7), which is the plan's own
   stated stopping point.
